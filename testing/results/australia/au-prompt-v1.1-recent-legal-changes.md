## Analysis of Legal Issues

### 1. Whether the Commonwealth minimum-age regime in the Online Safety Act 2021 (Cth) is likely to apply to your service as an age-restricted social media platform

**Issue:**  
Whether, under **Commonwealth** law, your online service is likely to be an **age-restricted social media platform**.

**Rule:**  
Part 4A of the **Online Safety Act 2021 (Cth)** establishes that providers of certain social media platforms must take reasonable steps to prevent children below the minimum age from having accounts: **Online Safety Act 2021 (Cth) s 63A**.

Section 63C provides that an **age-restricted social media platform** includes an electronic service where:

> the sole purpose, or a significant purpose, of the service  
> is to enable online social interaction between 2 or more end - users;

> the service allows end - users to link to, or interact  
> with, some or all of the other end - users;

> the service allows end - users to post material on the  
> service;

together with any other conditions set by legislative rules, unless the service falls within an exclusion: **Online Safety Act 2021 (Cth) s 63C(1)(a), (6)**.

Section 63C(2) states that online social interaction includes interaction enabling end-users to share material for **social purposes**. Section 63C(3) requires advertising-related purposes to be disregarded when assessing whether enabling social interaction is the sole or a significant purpose: **Online Safety Act 2021 (Cth) ss 63C(2), (3)**.

A service is excluded if **none** of its material is accessible to, or delivered to, one or more end-users in Australia, or if it is specified in legislative rules as excluded: **Online Safety Act 2021 (Cth) s 63C(6)**.

**Application:**  
On your facts, the service appears likely to satisfy the core statutory description.

- Users are **logged in** and can **create profiles**.
- They can **post videos**, which strongly suggests users can **post material on the service**.
- They can **follow one another**, which is a strong indicator that end-users can **link to, or interact with, some or all other end-users**.
- A **personalised recommendation feed** is not itself decisive, but in context it is consistent with a platform built around ongoing user-to-user social engagement.
- The service is **available in Australia**, so the statutory exclusion for services whose material is not accessible to users in Australia does not apply.

The strongest point in favour of coverage is that a **significant purpose** of the service appears to be enabling online social interaction between users. The combination of profiles, following, posting videos and interaction architecture points toward a social platform rather than a purely transactional, business or one-way publishing service.

The Act notes that online social interaction does **not** include, for example, online business interaction, and that social purposes do **not** include business purposes: **Online Safety Act 2021 (Cth) s 63C(1) note 1; s 63C(2) note**. On the facts given, your service does not appear to be characterised primarily as a business-only or enterprise collaboration service.

There is, however, one qualification. Section 63C also allows additional inclusion or exclusion by **legislative rules**. If March 2026 rules specify further conditions, specify your class of service, or exclude your class of service, that could alter the position. On the information available, there is no identified exclusion that would obviously remove a conventional profile-based, user-posting, follow-based video service from the regime.

**Conclusion:**  
Your service is **likely** to fall within the statutory concept of an **age-restricted social media platform** under **Online Safety Act 2021 (Cth) s 63C**, subject to any operative legislative rules in force in March 2026 that add conditions or create a relevant exclusion.

### 2. If the regime applies, what reasonable-steps obligation falls on the platform provider in relation to users under the minimum age, and whether parental consent avoids that obligation

**Issue:**

Section 63J proceeds on the basis that a provider may contravene **section 63D** by **failing to take reasonable steps to prevent age-restricted users having accounts**: **Online Safety Act 2021 (Cth) s 63J(a)**. That confirms the obligation is directed to the **provider**, not to children or parents.

The Act also contains privacy-related controls tied to this regime, including contraventions concerning collection of information and use of identification material or services, referenced in **Online Safety Act 2021 (Cth) ss 63DA, 63DB, 63J(b)–(c)**.

**Application:**
