If the Commissioner is satisfied that the provider has contravened **s 63D** or **s 63DA(1)**, the Commissioner may prepare a statement, give it to the provider, and publish it on the Commissioner's website if appropriate: **Online Safety Act 2021 (Cth) s 63J**.

**Application:** On your facts, the most immediate exposure is under **s 63D** if your service is an age-restricted social media platform and you allowed under-16 users to retain accounts rather than taking reasonable steps to prevent that.  
A second exposure may arise if, in trying to enforce age restrictions, the platform collected categories of information later specified by legislative rules as prohibited for that compliance purpose. That issue depends on the content of the March 2026 rules.

The available material does not define **reasonable steps** exhaustively. So the assessment will turn on what steps the platform actually implemented and what the legislative rules required or prohibited at the relevant time. However, a model that affirmatively permits below-minimum-age accounts on parental consent appears, at least prima facie, exposed to an argument that the provider did not take reasonable steps to **prevent** such accounts.

In addition to civil penalties, there is reputational and regulatory risk because the Commissioner may issue and publish a statement recording the contravention.

**Conclusion:** Your principal exposure is a **civil penalty under Online Safety Act 2021 (Cth) s 63D** of **30,000 penalty units**, with additional possible exposure under **Online Safety Act 2021 (Cth) s 63DA(1)** if prohibited information was collected for compliance purposes.
